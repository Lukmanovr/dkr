The graphlet degree vector is a census: for every small shape — wedge, triangle, 4-path, 3-star, square — and every position a node can hold in it (an orbit), count how many copies of that shape contain the node at that position, with a copy counting only when no extra edge sits among its nodes. Degree is the first entry of that census and the clustering coefficient is a ratio of two more, so P and Q, with three friends apiece of whom exactly two are friends with each other, are identical on both — yet the census tells them apart three times over, because P's third friend circles back through a square while Q's leads away down a path. The price is written in the enumeration: this widget checks every 3- and 4-node subset, and the number of k-node subsets grows like nk, which is why exact counts stop at five-node shapes in practice and why §5 replaces the census with an iterative fingerprint that costs one pass over the edges per round. Try this: select 4-path · middle and watch Q's row read 2 against P's 1, with each counted path shaded on the graph. Then click node 7 to make it P: its degree and clustering match Q's as well, and the verdict holds because the census still finds three rows that differ. Click node 3 or node 4 instead and the band changes to "same degree, different clustering" — the census is the tool you reach for once the cheaper features have failed, not before.
