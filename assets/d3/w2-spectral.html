Interactive · Zero eigenvalues count the pieces; the Fiedler vector finds the seam

Two facts about the Laplacian L = D − A, computed live from the edge list. First, eigenvalue 0 appears once per connected component: the cast graph has one, and deleting C–E and C–F makes it two. Second, on a connected graph the eigenvector of λ2 — the Fiedler vector — thresholded at zero cuts the graph where it is cheapest: {A, B, D} from {C, E, F} for the price of 2 edges, the best any 3 | 3 split can do, and on the barbell exactly at the bridge. This is relaxation at work: allow the 0/1 labels to become real numbers and an NP-hard balanced-cut problem turns into an eigenvector you can compute in milliseconds. Try this: switch to barbell and read λ2 — it drops from 0.63 to 0.35, and the one dashed edge is the bridge — then to two pieces and count the labels reading 0.00: there are two, and the vector shown is constant on each piece, which is exactly why a disconnected graph has no single Fiedler vector to threshold.
