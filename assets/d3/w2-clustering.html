Interactive · One node's clustering, and two ways to average it

Clustering is a fraction: for a node u, Cu counts the pairs of u's neighbors that are themselves joined by an edge and divides by all C(du, 2) pairs — and the diagonal of A3 hands you the numerator, because every triangle at u is a closed three-step walk taken both ways round. The two "global" versions do not agree: the average clustering C̄ is the plain mean of the local values, one vote per node, while the transitivity T is three times the number of triangles divided by the number of wedges, one vote per two-edge path, so the hubs that own most wedges dominate it. The second view shows the two verdicts crossing — C̄ far above T on the windmill, far below it on the clique with leaves — and on heavy-tailed real graphs the gap can reach an order of magnitude, so a reported "clustering of 0.6" is not a claim until the definition is named. Try this: click D, whose single friend leaves no pair to check, and read how the convention reports it; then click A and confirm by eye that exactly one of its three friend-pairs is closed, giving CA = 1/3 with (A3)[A,A] = 2.
