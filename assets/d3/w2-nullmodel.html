Interactive · Modularity's null model: cut, re-splice, count
·

The null model is a physical procedure, not a formula: cut every edge in half so each person keeps as many loose stubs as their degree, splice the 2m = 14 stubs back together uniformly at random, and ask how many edges a given pair ends up with on average. A and C own 7 of the 14 stubs between them, so they collide by chance in almost every rewiring and their one real edge is worth only +0.143 above expectation; D and E own 3, almost never meet, and so an edge between them would be strong evidence of togetherness — that is why hubs earn a community almost no credit. The "observed minus expected" readout is exactly one entry of the modularity matrix BQ = A − ddT/2m (the modularity matrix), built here from scratch. Try this: press rewire once a few times and count the self-loops and doubled edges the splicing produces — they are allowed, which is why the lecture writes dudv/2m with an "-ish"; then press rewire 100× and watch the empirical bar settle within a few hundredths of the dashed exact value 12/13 ≈ 0.923, a little above the 0.857 the formula promises, and switch to track D–E to see both bars shrink to about 0.15.
