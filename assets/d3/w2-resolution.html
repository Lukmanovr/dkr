Every clique on this ring is a perfect community, yet once the ring is long enough the partition that fuses neighbors in pairs scores higher: on 24 triangles the ground truth reaches Q = 0.708 while the merged pairs reach 0.792, so a maximum-modularity optimizer would fuse them on purpose. This is the resolution limit: Q judges every partition against a null model whose scale is set by the whole graph's m, so communities with fewer than about √(2m) internal edges (13.9 here, against the 3 inside a triangle) can be swallowed — and that threshold grows with the graph, not with the community. The √(2m) figure is a scale, not a sharp line; the chart marks the exact crossover for this ring, c = 8 for triangles, 14 for cliques of four and 22 for cliques of five. When small groups are the object of interest, scan a resolution parameter γ (both networkx implementations accept one) or run Leiden with a different objective such as the Constant Potts Model, which has no such limit, rather than trusting plain Q.
