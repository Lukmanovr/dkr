Interactive · The ceiling: what each pipeline can see

Four pipelines on the same task — predicting which of seven topics a Cora paper belongs to — with the same classifier wherever a classifier is fitted. Seven structural statistics barely clear chance, because how central or tight-knit a paper is says almost nothing about what it is about. Adding a one-hot of the node's Louvain community lifts the identical classifier to 62%: under homophily, topic lives in where you sit, so the jump comes from position, not from a better statistic. That 62% is also a ceiling — a hard partition is a lossy, task-blind compression of position, and nothing in the pipeline ever reads the papers' words — which is why the Week 6 model, computing on structure and words jointly, reaches 81%. Try this: flip between the statistics only and + community one-hot rows and read the chips: the only chip that changes is position, and the bar moves 43 points. Then select the GCN row and watch words fill for the first time — that chip, not a better statistic, is what the remaining 19 points are made of.
