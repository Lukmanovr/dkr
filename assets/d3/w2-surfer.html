The plain fixed point r = Mr is only as good as the web it runs on. Delete P6's outlinks and the corresponding column of M is all zeros, so every step discards whatever mass reached P6 and the only fixed point left is r = 0; wire P5 and P6 into a closed pair and mass that enters can never leave, so the trap ends up with nearly everything even though it receives just one third of the hub's clicks. Teleport is not a patch bolted onto the theorem — it is what makes the theorem true: with β = 0.85 the update r ← βMr + (1−β)·1/n is a contraction on the probability simplex, which is exactly why the total stays 1, why dangling mass is spread rather than lost, and why the trap gives up 15% of what it holds at every step. Try this: choose spider trap and press run 30 steps with β = 1 — the trap reads 0.978. Now select β = 0.85 and press run 30 steps again: the trap drains to about 0.58 while Σ r shows 1.000 throughout. Notice that the trap still holds more than its fair share (two pages of six would be 0.333) — teleport bounds a trap's advantage, it does not erase it, and that is the honest reading of the damping factor.
