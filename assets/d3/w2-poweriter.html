X and Y tie even though X has a single inlink, because that inlink is Y's only outlink and so delivers all of Y's mass, while Y's two inlinks bring only half of X's mass plus Z's smaller share; the fixed point is precisely the balance at which what flows in equals what is already there. The chart on the right shows the distance to r* shrinking by roughly ×0.7 per step, tracking the modulus 1/√2 ≈ 0.71 of M's second eigenvalue, in a stair-step pattern because on this small cycle the walk rotates as well as contracts. Try this: switch the start to all on X, so r⁽⁰⁾ = (1, 0, 0), and step twice. The trajectory is completely different, (0, 1/2, 1/2) and then (1/2, 1/2, 0), yet run lands on the same (0.400, 0.400, 0.200): the fixed point does not depend on where you start, which is what the proposition guarantees and why nobody worries about warm-starting PageRank.
